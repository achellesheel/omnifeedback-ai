Working Paper — Applied ML Systems

Detecting and Correcting Synthetic-Data Overfitting in a Multi-Model Customer Feedback Triage Pipeline

Pranav Chaturvedi
OmniFeedback AI Project · github.com/achellesheel/omnifeedback-ai
Abstract

We present OmniFeedback AI, an end-to-end customer-feedback triage system combining a transactional data warehouse, unsupervised aspect clustering, a bidirectional LSTM (BiLSTM) urgency regressor, transformer-based entity extraction and summarization, and a prompt-engineered generative resolution copilot. During development, an initial synthetic training corpus produced apparently ideal results — classifier macro-F1 of 1.000 and K-Means silhouette of 1.000 — which we show to be an artifact of data construction (9 unique verbatim training sentences) rather than evidence of model competence. We describe two concrete methodological fixes: (1) a hardened data generator introducing template-based phrase composition, deliberately ambiguous boundary cases, and simulated label noise, and (2) replacement of naive silhouette-maximizing cluster-count selection with a Kneedle-style maximum-curvature elbow method, which we show is necessary because template-structured text produces silhouette curves that do not plateau within a practically searchable range. We report full before/after metrics, an error analysis distinguishing genuine model failure from boundary-case ambiguity and injected label noise, and a data limitation (statistically non-significant channel–urgency association, χ²=4.18, p=0.840) traced to the generator's independent metadata assignment. We argue that this progression — from suspiciously perfect metrics, through root-cause diagnosis, to an honestly-reported and independently verifiable "before/after" comparison shipped directly in the production application — is itself a reusable methodological pattern for applied ML projects trained partly or wholly on synthetic data.

1. Introduction

Enterprises process feedback across many uncoordinated channels — app store reviews, support tickets, social posts, call transcripts — and manual triage is too slow to catch emerging incidents (billing defects, outages, product regressions) before they compound. Automated triage systems typically combine several model families: unsupervised topic discovery, supervised or regression-based urgency scoring, transformer-based entity/summarization tooling, and increasingly, LLM-based response drafting. Each component is usually evaluated in isolation, against whatever training data was available at the time — and when that data is synthetic, a specific failure mode emerges that this paper documents concretely: near-perfect offline metrics that do not indicate generalizable model competence, but rather trivial separability introduced by the data-generation process itself.

This paper documents that failure mode as it occurred during development of OmniFeedback AI, the concrete diagnostic process used to identify it, and the two fixes applied — a hardened data generator and a corrected cluster-count selection method — together with the resulting, more modest but credible metrics. Unlike a purely retrospective case study, both the "before" (V1) and "after" (V2) configurations are preserved and independently re-runnable in the shipped system, allowing a reader to reproduce the comparison rather than take the paper's word for it.

2. Related Work

Bidirectional LSTMs [Schuster & Paliwal, 1997; Hochreiter & Schmidhuber, 1997] remain a strong, low-resource-friendly baseline for sequence regression tasks relative to transformer fine-tuning. K-Means cluster-count selection commonly relies on the silhouette coefficient [Rousseeuw, 1987] or inertia-based elbow inspection; the Kneedle algorithm [Satopaa et al., 2011] formalizes elbow detection as the point of maximum distance from the chord connecting a curve's endpoints, and we apply it here to a metric where "higher is better" (silhouette) rather than its original "lower is better" (cost/inertia) framing. Named entity recognition and abstractive summarization use pretrained BERT [Devlin et al., 2019] and BART [Lewis et al., 2020] respectively. The generative copilot component draws on chain-of-thought prompting [Wei et al., 2022] and few-shot in-context learning [Brown et al., 2020]. Optimization throughout uses Adam [Kingma & Ba, 2015].

3. System Architecture

Raw feedback text is cleaned by a regular-expression-based ingestor (HTML/URL/mention stripping, repeated-character collapsing, hashtag-word retention) and loaded into a SQLite star schema (Fact_Feedback plus Dim_User, Dim_Channel, Dim_Product) under explicit transaction boundaries with foreign-key and check constraints enforced at the database level. Three parallel modeling paths consume the cleaned corpus: (a) TF-IDF vectorization feeding K-Means clustering and Logistic Regression / Random Forest baseline classifiers, (b) a custom PyTorch BiLSTM continuous urgency regressor, and (c) HuggingFace BERT-NER and BART-summarization pipelines invoked on demand. A prompt-engineered generative copilot consumes the BiLSTM's urgency output and drafts a structured JSON resolution (root cause, risk level, recommended action, customer-facing reply), with a pluggable backend that falls back to a deterministic rule-based generator when no LLM API credential is configured, guaranteeing schema-valid output in both cases.

4. Methodology

4.1 Data: two generator variants

V1 (naive) reproduces a common quick-prototype pattern: a small fixed list of verbatim phrases (5 negative, 4 positive) sampled uniformly at a flat 40% negative rate, yielding exactly 9 unique training sentences across 4,000 records. V2 (hardened) composes sentences from parameterized templates with randomized fill-words (212 unique surface forms across the same 4,000 records), adds a fourth "ambiguous" template class whose target urgency score is sampled to deliberately straddle the 0.5 escalation threshold, and applies label noise (label and score jointly flipped) to approximately 3% of records to simulate annotator disagreement. Both variants otherwise share identical downstream code — the same ingestion, database schema, training scripts, and evaluation methodology — so any metric difference is attributable to the data construction alone.

4.2 Cluster-count selection

An initial pass selected K by argmax over silhouette scores swept across k ∈ [2, 12]. On V1, this scored silhouette = 1.000 for every k ≥ 9 — the point at which cluster count reaches the corpus's true number of unique strings, at which point each cluster contains only exact duplicates and the metric is saturated rather than informative. Because this curve does not plateau below the search boundary, a naive argmax silently selects whatever the largest tested k happens to be. We replaced this with a Kneedle-style procedure: normalize both axes to [0,1], and select the k with maximum perpendicular distance from the chord connecting the first and last sampled points — a method that degrades gracefully on both the degenerate V1 curve and the smoothly diminishing-returns V2 curve.

4.3 BiLSTM training protocol

A single fixed-seed split (70% train / 15% validation / 15% test) is drawn once before any model sees the data. The regressor (embedding dim 128 → bidirectional LSTM, 64 units/direction → dropout p=0.3 → linear + sigmoid) is trained with Adam (weight decay 1e-5) under MSE loss with early stopping on validation MSE (patience=4). All reported metrics are computed on the held-out test split only.

On V2, a post-hoc threshold sweep (macro-F1 evaluated at 181 candidate thresholds from 0.05–0.95) found an optimum at t=0.48 (F1=0.8285) versus t=0.5 (F1=0.8260) — a 0.3-point gain, indicating the default threshold was already near-optimal rather than leaving material accuracy unclaimed. Predicted-vs-ground-truth urgency correlation on V2 was r=0.869. A chi-squared test of independence between channel and a three-level urgency bucket returned χ²=4.18, p=0.840 (8 d.f.) — no significant association, traced in Section 6 to the generator's independent metadata assignment rather than a modeling failure.

6. Discussion

Error composition, not just error rate. Manual review of V2's misclassifications shows false negatives concentrated almost entirely in the deliberately-ambiguous template class (target urgency 0.35–0.6, straddling the decision boundary by construction) rather than distributed across obviously-urgent text — i.e., the model is landing on the wrong side of an intentionally hard boundary, not failing to comprehend clear signal. At least one false positive was traced directly to the label-noise injection: a canonically critical-template sentence ("System latency is terrible after the server migration, nothing works.") carried a corrupted low ground-truth label, meaning the model's prediction was semantically correct against a deliberately noised target. This distinction — model error versus dataset-construction artifact — is only recoverable because the noise-injection process was logged and auditable, which we recommend as standard practice whenever label noise is intentionally introduced for robustness testing.

A metric-selection pitfall, caught by the numbers not making sense. An initial post-hoc threshold-optimization pass optimized sklearn's precision_recall_curve output, which tracks positive-class F1 only; comparing that value against the macro-F1 used everywhere else in the pipeline produced a nominal "best" threshold that scored worse than the existing default — a direct contradiction, since a true optimum cannot underperform a candidate already inside its search space under the same metric. Re-deriving the sweep against macro-F1 directly resolved the contradiction. We surface this as a caution: threshold or hyperparameter "optimization" against a metric silently different from the one used for reporting is a plausible, easy-to-miss error whose only reliable tell is a result that doesn't add up.

Independence as a limitation, not a null result to explain away. The non-significant channel/urgency association is not evidence that channel is generally uninformative for urgency prediction — it is evidence that this generator assigns channel independently of content by construction. We flag this distinction explicitly because a plausible failure mode in applied ML reporting is treating a synthetic dataset's design choices as findings about the world.

7. Limitations

All quantitative results in this paper concern synthetic, template-generated text; while V2's design specifically targets known synthetic-data failure modes (verbatim repetition, absent boundary ambiguity, noiseless labels), it remains a simplification of real multi-channel enterprise feedback. Real Yelp and Sentiment140 samples were retrieved and validated as loadable during this project but not yet run through the full training/evaluation methodology described here — the natural next step to test whether the V2-vs-V1 pattern (and the Kneedle fix's necessity) replicates on genuinely human-authored text rather than templated synthetic text. The GenAI copilot's local fallback backend is intentionally rule-based rather than learned, and its output quality was not evaluated against the optional Claude-backed path in this paper.

8. Conclusion

We documented a complete diagnostic cycle — suspiciously perfect initial metrics, root-cause identification (verbatim-phrase data construction), and two concrete, independently verifiable fixes (a hardened generator and a Kneedle-based elbow method) — inside a working, deployed multi-model feedback-triage system. Both the "before" and "after" configurations remain live in the shipped application specifically so the comparison in Table 1 is reproducible by a reader rather than merely asserted by the authors.
