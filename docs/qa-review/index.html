QA & Product Review

End-to-End Flow, QA Findings & Business Justification

A QA/PM-style walkthrough of OmniFeedback AI as it exists today — written against the actual shipped product, not an aspirational spec. Live at the deployed app.

1. End-to-end user journeys

Support Manager
  1. Pastes a ticket into Inference Playground → urgency score in under a second
  2. If HIGH/CRITICAL, moves to GenAI Copilot for root cause + draft reply
  3. Sends the reviewed draft, logs the action
Friction: text doesn't carry over between the two pages yet — queued in the roadmap.
Product Manager
  1. Checks Executive Overview for critical rate, channel trends, aspect volume
  2. Opens Clustering Explorer — elbow chart justifies K, not just asserts it
  3. Checks Statistical Tests before trusting any channel-based intuition
Gap: no exportable report snapshot — screen-share only, for now.
Technical Reviewer
  1. Opens Model Comparison first — it's page 0
  2. Sees V1 hit F1=1.0, sees why that's a red flag, not a win
  3. Scrolls to the V2-vs-V3 section, sees the real 13× separation gap on real-world sentences
This journey is the strongest single feature for a skeptical audience — it now covers two independent, verifiable fixes, not one.

3. Business value, honestly framed

Crisis detection speed
Urgency scored in well under 350ms; catches ~80% of true critical tickets at the deployed 0.5 threshold (real confusion-matrix number, not a marketing claim of near-100%).
Generalization to real customer language (V3)
V2's from-scratch BiLSTM scored real-world critical and positive feedback almost identically (0.02 separation) whenever phrasing left its narrow training templates — found live, not in a test. V3 (transfer-learned DistilBERT, ~21% of parameters retrained) raised that separation to 0.30, ~13× larger, with zero additional training data. This is the difference between a demo that only works on cherry-picked examples and one that holds up under real scrutiny.
Clustering for roadmap prioritization
Works, but currently capped by the channel/aspect-independence finding above — clusters describe how people phrase feedback, not yet what product area it's about. Worth stating to a prospective client rather than overselling.
Executive summarization
BART live-tested and confirmed coherent — genuinely condenses a batch of tickets to a short brief, verified rather than assumed.
GenAI resolution copilot
Zero API cost via the local backend, transparent upgrade to Claude — directly answers the common enterprise objection: "we don't want per-token costs during a pilot."
The platform is credible to demo today, with three caveats a presenter should state proactively: (1) clustering/channel findings are currently constrained by synthetic-data design, not model capability; (2) the GenAI local backend is intentionally simple by design, not a limitation to hide — its value is the zero-cost default and clean upgrade path; and (3) V3's CRITICAL threshold is calibrated to two specific training patterns (data loss, account compromise), not a general "how bad does this sound" scale — a severe complaint outside those patterns will land HIGH, which is expected behavior, not a defect, but worth saying before a reviewer discovers the boundary themselves.
